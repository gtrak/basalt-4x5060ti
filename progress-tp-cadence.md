# TP rendezvous-cadence probe — build report and predicted table

Tool: `tools/tp_cadence_probe.cu`. Standalone `nvcc` program; not a CMake target, not linked into the engine.
Question it prices: at TP=4, can cross-card attention/reduce work hide under MoE-cover compute at 4-deep
pipelining, or does max-of-4 rendezvous inflation expose the wire?

contended with real inference in both directions. They are retracted below and are not evidence.

## Two bugs found in this session, both fixed, both CPU-side

1. **Host dereference of a device pointer.** `release_rank` walked `r.staging[c][i]`, but `r.staging[c]` is the
   device-side peer-pointer table that `add_wait` indexes per element. Freeing through it is a host read of device
   memory and segfaulted on the way out. The peer slots are now freed from the host mirror `staging_h`. The table
   is also allocated `ranks` wide and was being freed `kMaxRanks` wide; `Rank::staging_ranks` records the width.
2. **A per-unit figure subtracted from a whole-window figure.** `window_cover` was divided by the unit count while
   `window_reduce` and `window_both` were whole-window totals, so `exposed = both - cover` understated the cover by
   a factor of `units`. The RESULT line now prints the unit count and the per-unit cover beside the totals, and a
   selftest block asserts that cover, wire and both are whole-window totals over the same unit count.

## Retracted: the "anti-additive co-scheduling" number

An earlier point printed `co-scheduling 326 ms` — the pair running far worse than cover+wire added — which read as
"the spin-wait `add_wait` holds SMs, so the cover kernel cannot get slots". **That measurement is void.** It was
taken on cards busy with live inference, and it was produced through the units bug above, which by itself accounts
for the whole effect (true exposed was on the order of 10 ms, not 367 ms).

The SM-contention mechanism remains **a hypothesis the window tests, not a result.** `coset_ms` is now reported as
a first-class quantity precisely so the window can settle it on idle cards. If the window reproduces a large
positive `coset_ms`, that is a real and important finding: it would mean SM-driven reduce and SM-resident cover
cannot coexist regardless of fabric rate, which would sink TP=4 hiding on its own.

`hidden` is the fraction of the wire absorbed under the cover. `infl+Jms` is how much longer the barrier runs when
one rank is sustained `J` ms slow — a separate coefficient, never folded into `hidden`, because a straggler's cost
is compute time and must not be divided by the wire budget. Rates are the RUN 24 fabric pool.

Two things to watch in the measured grid:

- **depth 2 and depth 4 are predicted IDENTICAL in every row.** A measured gap between them is the max-of-R
  rendezvous the model does not contain — the most informative disagreement the run can produce.
- **the uniform schedule biases hidden upward.** Same bytes spread over all 24 positions reads `hidden 100.0%`
  against the two-channel `14.0%` — a `+86.0` point bias. The two-channel number is the one to decide on.

Decode cadence, 4 ranks: 96 rounds x 4 KiB at 0.64 GiB/s is 0.006 ms/round, 0.57 ms/window against the 19.4 ms
budget. The bandwidth floor alone says the budget is not binding; whether SM or copy-engine pushes, and the 3-peer
straggler spread, are what the run settles.

## The 70% line, re-derived against the corrected ledger

The original `>=70%` kill line was derived against the uniform schedule, which the table above shows overstates
hiding by up to 86 points. Against the two-channel schedule and the corrected bytes, **no row of the predicted grid
reaches 70%**, and partial-merge — the channel that still has pool capacity — tops out at 54.6% at 2 ranks and
14.0% at 4. Row-ship has no pool capacity at all, so it is not judged against any threshold here; the harness
reports it and the decision stays with the reader. The tool deliberately reports hidden fraction and exposed ms and
**does not** encode a verdict in its exit status.

## WINDOW RESULTS (10-31, cards idle, server down; supervisor-run)

The decisive points at the REAL ratios (the sweep defaults were unrepresentative —
cover 2.5 ms/layer vs the engine's ~50; tokens 2048 vs 8192):

**PREFILL GATE: FAILED.** partial-merge r=4 d=4 tokens=8192 cover=49.9ms/layer:
wire 39.6 ms/layer, HIDDEN 42.7% (model 52.0), ceiling = cover/(cover+wire) = 56% —
the 70% kill line is UNREACHABLE at this wire:cover ratio. The wire adds ~46% to the
layer wall (72.6 vs 49.9 ms). Projection: the TP=4 lockstep ~73-100 ms/chunk effective
vs the split's ~45 ms/chunk (32.6 s / 720 layer-chunks, 2-deep pipelined) — the split
wins ~1.4-2x. The streaming (~27 ms/layer-card at TP=4) contends with the wire on the
shared uplink (the owner's face-contention correction) — it cannot hide under it.

**DECODE GATE: PASSED.** cover 0.085 ms/round: wire 42 us/round at 4 ranks, HIDDEN
90.3% (SM) / 92.6% (copy-engine — within noise), exposed 4 us/round. The 3-peer
straggler term: spread 0.000-0.006 ms, inflation 1.00x. TP=4's decode coordination
would NOT inherently break the 19.4 ms windows — but decode alone cannot justify the
build (tp2's decode is zero-wire; the TP=4 decode gain is second-order).

**HYPOTHESES SETTLED:**
- max-of-4 straggler: REFUTED (inflation 1.00x everywhere, spread <= 0.15 ms even
  with tier blobs and at 8192)
- SM-contention (the spin-wait starving the cover): REFUTED (co-scheduling NEGATIVE
  — the overlap works; the earlier +326 ms was bug 2 x live contention, retracted)
- row-ship: dead by capacity AND by hiding (2.8% at 4 ranks, 5.3 s wire/window)
- the uniform-schedule bias: measured +86 points (the model's own number) — the
  two-channel schedule is the honest one

**VERDICT (supervisor, per TP4-SKETCH.md's four-gate rule): TP=4 STAYS SHELVED.**
Gates (a) and (d) failed on measurement; the decode gate passed but is insufficient.
The sglang prior was regime-mismatched (bandwidth-bound on independent links vs
compute-bound on a shared pool). The morning's topology statement: the split for
prefill, tp2 for decode/latency, TP=4 measured-and-shelved.
